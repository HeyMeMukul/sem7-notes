6.1 Six Sigma and TQM

Syllabus: Six Sigma for Software Engineering, Total Quality Management, achieving software quality.

Q1Explain in detail the Six Sigma characteristics (strategy of software quality assurance).

6 marks★★★★★ 6×

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q7(b) [6] · 2022 Nov Q8(a) [6] · 2023 May Q7(a) [6] · 2024 May Q7(b) [6] · 2025 May Q7(a) [6] · 2025 Nov Q7(c) [6]

Definition: Six Sigma is a data-driven, customer-focused, process-improvement methodology that reduces variation and defects to 3.4 defects per million opportunities (DPMO) — a 99.99966% success rate.

1. Sigma levels

LevelDefects per millionYieldMeaning
1σ690,00031%Chaotic
2σ308,00069%Poor
3σ66,80093.3%Average industry
4σ6,21099.38%Good
5σ23399.977%Excellent
6σ3.499.99966%World class

2. Characteristics

  1. Customer-focused: quality is defined by the customer's critical-to-quality (CTQ) requirements.
  2. Data-driven: decisions use measured data, not opinions; “In God we trust, all others bring data.”
  3. Process-focused: defects are symptoms; the process is the root cause and must be improved.
  4. Project-based: improvement happens through defined projects with charters, owners and savings targets.
  5. Statistical: uses control charts, capability indices (Cp, Cpk), Pareto and hypothesis tests to reduce variation.
  6. Breakthrough improvement: targets large reductions (e.g. 50% defects), not small incremental changes.
  7. Belt system: Champions, Master Black Belts, Black Belts and Green Belts lead projects and training.
  8. Top-management commitment: leadership selects projects, allocates resources and reviews results.

3. DMAIC — the Six Sigma method

PhaseActivitySoftware example
DefineDefine problem, customer, goal and scope.“Reduce payment failures from 6% to 1%.”
MeasureMeasure current performance; validate data.Baseline 6.2% failures over 3 months.
AnalyseFind root causes with data.70% failures from gateway timeout on retry.
ImproveImplement and verify the fix.Add idempotency and retry with backoff; failure → 0.9%.
ControlStandardise and monitor.Control chart on failure rate; alert above 1.2%.

DMADV/DFSS is used for new products: Define, Measure, Analyse, Design, Verify.

Limitation: Six Sigma needs good data and trained people; it is weaker for innovative design and very small projects.

Q2Explain in detail Total Quality Management (TQM).

6 marks★★★ 3×

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q8(b) [6] · 2023 Nov Q7(a) [6] · 2025 May Q8(b) [6]

Definition: Total Quality Management is a management philosophy in which every member of the organisation continuously improves quality at every stage to meet customer needs. “Total” = everyone and everything; “Quality” = fitness for use; “Management” = leadership and continuous improvement.

1. Principles

  1. Customer focus: internal and external customers define quality.
  2. Top-management leadership: quality is a strategic goal, not a QA department task.
  3. Employee involvement: every person is responsible for quality; empowerment and teamwork.
  4. Process approach: improve the process, not blame the person.
  5. Continuous improvement: small, never-ending improvements (Kaizen) plus breakthrough projects.
  6. Fact-based decisions: use data and statistical tools.
  7. Supplier partnership: quality of bought-in components matters.
  8. Training and culture: invest in skills and a blame-free quality culture.

2. Elements / building blocks

ElementWhat it means in software
Quality policyTop management's written commitment and measurable objectives.
Quality planningDefine standards, reviews, tests and metrics for the project.
Quality controlReviews, testing and defect tracking.
Quality assuranceProcess definition, audits and training.
Quality improvementRoot-cause analysis, PDCA, benchmark and reuse.
People and cultureTraining, teams, recognition, no blame for reporting defects.

3. TQM in software — example

A company sets a policy “zero critical defects at release”. It trains developers in reviews, measures defect density, holds weekly quality circles, audits the test process and updates checklists after every escape. Every team member can stop a release on a quality concern.

4. Benefits and barriers

  • Benefits: fewer defects, lower rework, happier customers, better morale, continuous improvement.
  • Barriers: management indifference, short-term cost pressure, blame culture, lack of training, difficulty measuring soft benefits.

TQM vs Six Sigma: TQM is a broad management philosophy and culture; Six Sigma is a focused, data-driven project methodology with a specific defect target. TQM provides the culture; Six Sigma provides the method.

Q3Explain the activities / parameters / facts to achieve high software quality in detail.

5–8 marks★★★★ 4×

Asked in: 2023 May Q7(c) [5, parameters] · 2023 Nov Q8(b) [6, facts] · 2024 May Q7(a) [8, activities] · 2025 Nov Q8(c) [6, activities]

Software quality is not achieved by testing alone. It is built into the people, process, product and measurement across the lifecycle.

1. Activities across the lifecycle (use this table for 8 marks)

PhaseQuality activityExample / output
RequirementsElicit, review and baseline requirements; build a traceability matrix.Every requirement has an owner, acceptance criterion and test ID.
PlanningWrite the quality plan; estimate effort; identify risks and quality gates.Coverage target 80%, p95 < 2 s, review schedule.
DesignDesign reviews, checklists, prototyping, complexity analysis.Peer review finds a missing interface before coding.
CodingCoding standards, static analysis, peer review, unit tests.No merge without review and green unit tests.
TestingRisk-based test design, layered testing, regression, automation.Defects found before release; coverage measured.
ReleaseEntry/exit criteria, defect triage, UAT, installation and smoke tests.No open critical/high defects; signed acceptance.
MaintenanceImpact analysis, regression, root-cause analysis, metrics review.Recurring defects eliminated by process change.
OrganisationTraining, quality culture, standards, tools, audits, continual improvement.Certified QMS; trained testers; measured process capability.

2. Key parameters / facts

  1. Management commitment and a written quality policy.
  2. Clear, testable requirements and traceability.
  3. Defined process with entry/exit criteria and standards.
  4. Reviews and inspections at every phase — defects removed early.
  5. Layered testing with measurable coverage.
  6. Metrics — defect density, DRE, MTBF, coverage, customer satisfaction.
  7. Skilled, trained people and a blame-free culture.
  8. Tools and automation — version control, CI, static analysis, test automation.
  9. Configuration and change management — test the right version.
  10. Continual improvement — root-cause analysis and process updates.

One-line conclusion: quality is prevented in, not inspected in. The cheapest defect is the one stopped at the requirements review.

6.2 Ishikawa's 7 basic tools

Syllabus: Flow chart, checklists, Pareto diagrams, histogram, run charts, scatter diagrams, control chart, cause-effect diagram.

Q4Illustrate Ishikawa's 7 basic tools in detail.

6 marks★★ 2×

Asked in: 2023 Nov Q7(b) [6] · 2024 May Q8(c) [6, basic tools for quality control]

Kaoru Ishikawa popularised seven simple graphical tools that any team can use to find, analyse and control quality problems. They are called the 7 QC tools.

#ToolPurposeHow to read / example
1Cause-effect (fishbone) diagramFind root causes of a problem.Effect on the right; 6M branches: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment. Ask “why” five times.
2Check sheet / checklistCollect data in a structured way.Tally marks for defect type per day; shows frequency and location.
3Pareto chartSeparate the “vital few” from the “trivial many”.Bars in descending order + cumulative % line; 80% of defects from 20% of causes.
4HistogramShow the distribution of continuous data.Bars of response-time ranges; shape shows centre, spread, skew and outliers.
5Flow chartMap the process steps and decision points.Oval = start/end, rectangle = process, diamond = decision; find loops, waits and rework.
6Scatter diagramShow the relationship between two variables.Plot module size vs defect count; upward trend = positive correlation.
7Control chartMonitor whether a process is stable over time.Time-ordered points with mean, UCL and LCL at ±3σ; points outside limits = special cause.
8*Run chartShow trends, shifts and cycles over time.Time-ordered line with a median; no control limits. Often added as the eighth tool.

*Different textbooks list stratification instead of run chart. In SPPU papers, run chart and control chart are asked together, so learn both.

Quick example

A team sees 120 defects in a release. A check sheet records them by type. A Pareto shows 78% come from “payment” and “login”. A fishbone on payment finds the root cause: retry logic. A flow chart shows the retry loop. After the fix, a control chart tracks weekly payment defects and stays inside limits.

Q5Compare the Ishikawa's flow chart and Histogram tool.

6 marks★★★★ 4×

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q7(a) [6] · 2023 May Q7(b) [6] · 2025 May Q8(a) [6] · 2025 Nov Q7(b) [6]

PointFlow chartHistogram
PurposeShow the sequence of steps and decisions in a process.Show the distribution of measured data.
Data typeProcess steps, events, decisions (qualitative).Continuous numeric data (time, size, defects).
DisplayBoxes, arrows, diamonds, start/end ovals.Adjacent bars on a number line; x = class interval, y = frequency.
What it revealsRework loops, bottlenecks, missing steps, unclear ownership.Centre, spread, skew, bimodal shape, outliers, capability.
Question answered“How does the process actually work?”“How is the data distributed?”
Use in SQAMap the test/defect process; find unnecessary approvals and loops.Check response-time distribution or defect counts per module.
LimitationDoes not quantify variation.Does not show time order or root cause.

Example

Flow chart: “defect reported → triage → assign → fix → review → retest → close”, with a decision diamond “fix verified?” that loops back to assign.

Histogram: 500 login response times grouped into 0.5 s bins. Most fall in 1.0–1.5 s, with a small tail at 4–5 s — showing a slow subset that needs investigation.

One-line answer: a flow chart shows process flow; a histogram shows data distribution. Use the flow chart to find where work goes wrong, and the histogram to measure how much variation exists.

Q6Explain in brief: Histogram, Flowchart and Control chart.

3–6 marks★★ 2×

Asked in: 2024 May Q7(c) [3] · 2025 Nov Q8(b) [6] · 2023 Nov Q8(a) [6, flow chart + run chart + control chart]

ToolDefinitionHow to draw / readExample
HistogramA bar chart of the frequency distribution of continuous data.Divide data into equal class intervals; bar height = frequency. Look at centre, spread, skew and outliers.Distribution of 300 test execution times; most 2–3 s, a few 10 s.
Flow chartA diagram of the steps, decisions and flow of a process.Oval = start/end, rectangle = activity, diamond = decision, arrow = flow.Defect lifecycle from report to closure, including the retest loop.
Control chartA time-ordered chart with a centre line and upper/lower control limits (±3σ).Plot each sample; investigate points outside limits, runs of 7+ on one side, or trends.Weekly defect density with UCL 5.2 and LCL 0.4; a point at 7.1 is a special cause.

If the question asks flow chart + run chart + control chart (2023 Nov Q8a): define the flow chart and control chart as above, then add — a run chart is a time-ordered line graph with a median line but no control limits; it shows trends, shifts and cycles. It is the simpler cousin of the control chart.

Relationship: the flow chart shows how the process works, the histogram shows what the output looks like, and the run/control chart shows whether the process is stable over time.

Q7Compare Run charts and Control chart in detail.

5 marks★★ 2×

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q8(c) [5] · 2025 May Q8(c) [5]

PointRun chartControl chart
DefinitionLine graph of data in time order with a median line.Line graph with a mean and statistically calculated control limits (UCL/LCL).
LimitsNo control limits.UCL and LCL, usually mean ±3σ.
PurposeSpot trends, shifts, cycles and unusual patterns.Distinguish common-cause variation from special-cause variation.
Statistical basisSimple; no distribution assumption.Based on process variation and probability.
Decision powerIndicates a change; cannot confirm statistical control.Can decide whether the process is in control.
Cost / skillLow; anyone can draw it.Higher; needs data and formulas.

Example

Run chart: weekly defects 12, 10, 14, 9, 8, 11, 7 — a downward trend, but no limits, so we cannot say whether the last point is statistically unusual.

Control chart: same data with mean 10.1, UCL 16.2, LCL 4.0. All points lie inside, so the process is in control; the downward trend is common-cause variation, not a special event.

Memory hook: run chart = time + median; control chart = time + mean + 3σ limits.

6.3 SQA tasks, metrics and maintenance

Q8Can you explain how to maintain SQA?

5–6 marks★★★★ 4×

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q7(c) [5] · 2023 May Q8(a) [6] · 2025 May Q7(b) [6] · 2025 Nov Q7(a) [5]

Meaning: Maintaining SQA means keeping the quality system alive and effective after it is defined — auditing it, measuring it, updating it and improving it as the product and organisation change.

1. Activities

  1. Keep the process current: review the SDLC, standards and checklists after every major change; retire obsolete steps.
  2. Conduct audits: internal and external audits check that the process is actually followed; record nonconformities and corrective actions.
  3. Track metrics: defect density, DRE, coverage, defect leakage, MTBF, schedule variance. Compare with targets and trends.
  4. Review defects and root causes: Pareto and fishbone analysis; fix the process, not only the bug.
  5. Maintain the test assets: update test cases, data, automation scripts and environments as the product changes.
  6. Train and certify people: new tools, standards and domain knowledge; keep skills current.
  7. Manage tools and configuration: version control, CI, defect tracker, test-management tool; keep them integrated.
  8. Management review: periodic review of quality objectives, audit results, customer feedback and improvement actions (ISO 9001 clause 9).
  9. Continual improvement: PDCA — plan an improvement, implement, check the metric, standardise.
  10. Documentation: keep the quality manual, procedures and records up to date and accessible.

2. Example

A quarterly review finds defect leakage rose from 4% to 9%. The team audits the review process, finds that code reviews are being skipped under schedule pressure, adds a CI gate, retrains reviewers, and tracks leakage monthly until it returns below 5%.

One-line answer: maintain SQA by auditing, measuring, updating and improving the process and its assets — not by filing the quality manual once and forgetting it.

Q9Illustrate different tasks, goals and metrics in SQA.

5–6 marks★★ 2×

Asked in: 2023 May Q8(b) [6] · 2023 Nov Q8(c) [5]

1. SQA tasks

  • Prepare the SQA/quality plan and quality objectives.
  • Define and maintain processes, standards and templates.
  • Plan and conduct reviews, inspections and audits.
  • Oversee test planning and execution; check entry/exit criteria.
  • Collect and analyse metrics; publish quality reports.
  • Manage defects, change requests and configuration.
  • Conduct training and promote quality awareness.
  • Drive root-cause analysis and continual improvement.

2. SQA goals

  • Prevent defects rather than detect them late.
  • Meet functional and non-functional requirements.
  • Reduce cost of quality and rework.
  • Improve process capability and predictability.
  • Achieve customer satisfaction and compliance (ISO/CMM).

3. Metrics

MetricFormula / meaningTarget example
Defect densityDefects ÷ size (KLOC or function points)< 1 per KLOC
Defect removal effectiveness (DRE)Defects found before release ÷ total defects × 100> 95%
Defect leakageDefects found after release ÷ total defects × 100< 5%
Test coverageRequirements or code covered ÷ total × 100≥ 80% branch
Test case productivityTest cases designed ÷ person-dayProject baseline
MTBFTotal operating time ÷ number of failuresIncreasing
MTTRTotal repair time ÷ number of repairsDecreasing
Schedule / effort variance(Actual − planned) ÷ planned × 100Within ±10%
Customer satisfactionCSAT / NPS from surveys≥ 4.2/5 or NPS +30

Goal–question–metric (GQM): start from a goal (“reduce payment defects”), ask questions (“where do they occur?”), then choose metrics (“defects per payment module”). This keeps measurement purposeful.

Q10Explain Defect Removal Effectiveness (DRE) in detail. (Printed as “Detect Removal Effectiveness” in 2025 May.)

5 marks★★ 2×

Asked in: 2023 May Q8(c) [5] · 2025 May Q7(c) [5]

Definition: Defect Removal Effectiveness measures how well the quality process removes defects before delivery. It is the percentage of all defects that are found and fixed before the product reaches the customer.

DRE (%) = (defects found before delivery ÷ (defects found before delivery + defects found after delivery)) × 100

Equivalently: DRE = 1 − defect leakage.

1. Solved example

A release has 90 defects found in reviews and testing, and customers report 10 defects after release.

DRE = 90 ÷ (90 + 10) × 100 = 90%
Defect leakage = 10 ÷ 100 × 100 = 10%

Target for critical software is usually > 95%.

2. Phase-wise DRE

DRE can be calculated for each phase to find the weak gate:

PhaseDefects foundDefects escaping to next phasePhase effectiveness
Requirements review505050/100 = 50%
Design review302030/50 = 60%
Code review + unit test12812/20 = 60%
System test626/8 = 75%
Post-release2——

Start with 100 defects. Pre-release removal = 50 + 30 + 12 + 6 = 98; post-release = 2.

Overall DRE = 98 ÷ (98 + 2) = 98 ÷ 100 = 98%; leakage = 2%.

3. How to improve DRE

  • Add or strengthen reviews at the phase with the lowest effectiveness.
  • Use checklists based on past escape defects.
  • Increase test coverage and add regression tests for every escape.
  • Perform root-cause analysis on defects found after release.
  • Track DRE per release and set a quality gate (e.g. ≥ 95%).

Why it matters: DRE measures the process, not individual tester skill. A low DRE shows that defects are slipping through the quality gates and that prevention must improve.

Q11What do you understand by software maintenance? Explain in detail.

5 marks★ 1×

Asked in: 2023 Nov Q7(c) [5]

Definition: Software maintenance is the work done on a delivered product to correct defects, adapt to a changed environment, improve performance or prevent future problems. It usually consumes 50–70% of total lifecycle cost.

1. Types

TypePurposeExample
CorrectiveFix defects found after delivery.Patch a wrong tax calculation.
AdaptiveAdapt to a new OS, browser, database or law.Support Windows 11 or a new GST rate.
PerfectiveImprove performance, maintainability or add minor enhancements.Optimise a slow query; refactor a module.
PreventiveReduce future failure risk.Add logging, upgrade a library, add regression tests.

2. Maintenance process

  1. Request — bug report or change request.
  2. Impact analysis — which modules, interfaces, tests and data are affected.
  3. Approval and planning — priority, effort, release window.
  4. Change — modify code/design and update documentation.
  5. Testing — unit, integration, regression and UAT.
  6. Release and monitor — deploy, verify, close the request.

3. SQA role in maintenance

  • Enforce change control and configuration management.
  • Require impact analysis and regression testing for every change.
  • Track defect recurrence and MTBF.
  • Keep documentation and test cases current.
  • Use root-cause analysis to move from corrective to preventive work.

Example: a bank changes its interest rule (adaptive). The team analyses the affected calculation and report modules, changes the code, runs the full regression suite, and releases a patch with updated documentation.

Q13Write a note on Software Quality Assurance (SQA) plan.

5 marks★ 1×

Asked in: 2024 May Q8(b) [5]

Definition: An SQA plan defines how quality assurance will be performed for a specific project — the activities, responsibilities, standards, reviews, tests, metrics and records. IEEE 730 is the standard template.

Contents

  1. Purpose and scope — project, release and quality objectives.
  2. Reference documents — SRS, design, standards, contract.
  3. Management and organisation — QA roles, responsibilities, reporting.
  4. Documentation — which documents are produced, reviewed and stored.
  5. Standards, practices and metrics — coding standards, review checklists, coverage and defect targets.
  6. Reviews and audits — type, schedule, participants, entry/exit criteria.
  7. Test plan — levels, environments, data, tools, automation and acceptance criteria.
  8. Problem reporting and corrective action — defect workflow, severity, escalation.
  9. Tools and configuration management — version control, CI, defect tracker, test tool.
  10. Records, training and risks — audit evidence, skill plan, risk list.

Example quality gate

No release unless: 100% requirements traced, 80% branch coverage, zero open critical/high defects, performance targets met, and the test summary report is approved.

SQA plan vs quality plan: the quality plan is the broader project quality document; the SQA plan focuses on the assurance activities — reviews, audits, standards and evidence — that prove the process is working.

6.5 Syllabus-only SQA concepts

Not directly asked as a full question: software quality dilemma, elements of SQA, formal approaches to SQA, statistical SQA, product quality metrics and in-process quality metrics. Learn one short paragraph each.
TopicExam-ready answer
Software quality dilemmaQuality competes with cost, schedule and scope. Adding quality work raises short-term cost and time; skipping it raises long-term rework and failure cost. The dilemma is resolved by risk-based investment in prevention.
Elements of SQAStandards, reviews and audits, testing, metrics, defect management, configuration management, training, tools and documentation.
Formal approachesMathematical specification and verification (Z, VDM, B-method, model checking) to prove properties; used in safety-critical systems.
Statistical SQACollect defect data by phase, fit a distribution, find the phase with the highest defect rate and improve it; use control charts and Pareto analysis.
Product quality metricsDefect density, MTBF, customer-reported defects, reliability growth, severity distribution.
In-process quality metricsReview effectiveness, test coverage, defect removal effectiveness, phase yield, schedule/effort variance, test case productivity.
