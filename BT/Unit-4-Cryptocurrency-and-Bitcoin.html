4.1 Cryptocurrency basics

Q1What is cryptocurrency? Explain in detail.

6 marks★ 1×Unit 4

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q4(a) [6]

1. Definition

Cryptocurrency is digital or virtual money that uses cryptography to secure transactions, control the creation of new units and verify transfers. It runs on a decentralised blockchain, so no central bank or government issues it.

2. Key properties

  • Digital: exists only as data; no notes or coins.
  • Decentralised: a peer-to-peer network maintains the ledger; there is no single administrator.
  • Cryptographically secured: public/private keys and hash functions protect ownership.
  • Immutable: once confirmed, a transaction is very hard to reverse.
  • Pseudo-anonymous: addresses are visible, real names are not.
  • Programmable: some currencies (ETH) also run smart contracts.
  • Volatile: prices can move sharply because supply and demand are not managed by a central bank.

3. How it works

  1. A user creates a wallet with a public key (address) and a private key (signature).
  2. The sender signs a transaction with the private key and broadcasts it.
  3. Nodes check the signature and the balance.
  4. Miners/validators put the transaction into a block and reach consensus.
  5. The block is appended to the blockchain; the receiver sees the funds after confirmations.

4. Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages

Fast settlement, 24×7No central bank protection or insurance

5. Examples

Bitcoin (BTC), Ether (ETH), Tether (USDT), XRP, Binance Coin (BNB), Solana (SOL), Cardano (ADA), Monero (XMR).

Exam line: "Cryptocurrency is not issued by a central bank; it is created by mining or staking and recorded on a distributed ledger secured by cryptography."

Q2What is the relevance of crypto usage? Discuss with an example.

6 marks★ 1×Unit 4

Asked in: 2023 Nov Q4(b) [6]

1. Why businesses and users adopt crypto

  • New customers: crypto-paying users are often new customers and spend more per order.
  • Cheaper cross-border payments: no correspondent bank chain, so fees and settlement time fall.
  • New capital and liquidity: tokenised assets open new investor pools.
  • Programmable money: smart contracts enable automatic revenue sharing and reconciliation.
  • Treasury diversification: crypto can act as an inflation hedge or a balancing asset.
  • Competitive pressure: clients and vendors may want to pay or be paid in crypto.

2. Two adoption paths

PathHow it worksRisk / effort
Hands-offA third-party processor converts crypto to fiat at checkout; crypto never sits on the company balance sheet.Lowest effort; vendor fee; limited control.
Hands-onThe company holds crypto, manages its own wallet/keys and may use it for treasury and payments.Higher effort; custody, accounting and volatility risk.

3. Example

A coffee chain accepts BTC through a payment processor. The customer pays 0.0002 BTC; the processor instantly converts it to rupees and credits the chain's bank account. The chain gains new customers and avoids chargebacks, while never holding bitcoin itself. This is the hands-off model.

4. Relevance in India

  • Remittances from workers abroad can be cheaper and faster.
  • Small exporters can receive payment without heavy bank charges.
  • Investors use crypto as an asset class (taxed as a virtual digital asset).
  • However, price volatility and regulatory uncertainty limit everyday retail payments.

3. "Is cryptocurrency safe?" — balanced answer (2025 May Q3a)

Where it is safe

  • Cryptography: a transaction cannot be forged without the private key.
  • Immutability: confirmed blocks are very hard to rewrite.
  • Decentralisation: no single server to switch off.
  • Transparency: anyone can audit the ledger.
  • Self-custody: with a hardware wallet, the user controls the keys.

Where it is risky

  • Key loss: lost seed phrase = permanent loss.
  • Exchange failure: Mt. Gox, FTX and others froze user funds.
  • Phishing and malware: fake wallets and approvals drain accounts.
  • Smart-contract bugs: the 2016 DAO hack moved ~$50M of ETH.
  • Volatility and scams: pump-and-dump, rug pulls, fake tokens.
  • Regulation: rules change; tax and legal status vary by country.

Opinion line: "The technology is cryptographically safe, but the user journey is not. Safety depends on self-custody, a hardware wallet, verified contracts and small allocations. I would call crypto safe only for a user who understands key management, not for a first-time investor using an unregulated exchange."

Q4Describe Zerocoin and its successor Zerocash in detail.

8 marks★ 1×Unit 4

Asked in: 2025 May Q3(b) [8]

1. Why they exist

Bitcoin is pseudonymous, not anonymous. Every transaction is public, so chain analysis can link addresses and reveal identities. Zerocoin and Zerocash add financial privacy on top of a public ledger.

2. Zerocoin (2013)

  • Proposed by Miers, Garman, Green and Rubin as an extension to Bitcoin.
  • A user mints a zerocoin by locking some BTC and receives a zero-knowledge proof.
  • Later the user spends the zerocoin to redeem BTC at a new address. The proof shows the coin is valid without revealing which mint created it.
  • Hides: the link between the mint and the spend (the sender's identity).
  • Does not hide: the amount, or the receiver's address in full.
  • Fixed denominations and no trusted setup.

3. Zerocash (2014) — the successor

  • Proposed by Sasson, Chiesa, Garman, Green, Miers, Tromer and Virza.
  • Uses zk-SNARKs (zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge).
  • Supports shielded transactions: the sender, receiver and amount are hidden.
  • Users hold shielded addresses; the ledger stores only a commitment and a nullifier to stop double-spending.
  • Implemented as Zcash (ZEC), launched in 2016.
  • Cost: a trusted setup ceremony and heavier computation than Bitcoin.

4. Comparison

Year20132014

CryptographyZero-knowledge proofs (no trusted setup)zk-SNARKs (trusted setup)
Hides senderYesYes

4.3 Crypto wallets

Q5Explain in detail crypto wallets. / Discuss the concept of cryptowallets.

6 marks★★ 2×Unit 4

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q4(b) [6] · 2023 Nov Q3(a) [6]

1. Definition

A crypto wallet is software or hardware that stores the public and private keys needed to receive, send and manage cryptocurrency. The coins themselves live on the blockchain; the wallet controls the keys that prove ownership.

2. The two keys

  • Public key / address: like an account number. Share it to receive funds.
  • Private key: like a digital signature. It authorises spending. Never share it.
  • Seed phrase (recovery phrase): 12 or 24 words that can rebuild the wallet. Anyone with the phrase owns the funds.

        ├──► private key 1 ──► public key 1 ──► address 1

wallet = key manager, not a coin bank

3. Types of wallet (book's classification)

TypeWhere keys liveExamplesSecurityConvenience
Desktop walletInstalled on a laptop/PCElectrum, Exodus desktopHigh until the PC is infectedMedium
Mobile walletPhone appTrust Wallet, MetaMask mobileMedium; phone can be lostHigh
Online / web walletCloud, often third-partyExchange walletsLower; third party holds keysVery high
Hardware walletOffline USB-like deviceLedger, TrezorVery highLower

4. Two more useful classifications

  • Custodial vs non-custodial: who holds the private key — a third party or you. (See §4.4.)
  • Hot vs cold: whether the key is connected to the internet. (See §4.4.)

5. How a wallet sends a transaction

  1. Enter the receiver's address and amount.
  2. The wallet builds the transaction and shows the network fee.
  3. The user confirms; the wallet signs with the private key.
  4. The signed transaction is broadcast to the blockchain.
  5. The wallet shows a pending balance until confirmation.
Golden rule: "Not your keys, not your coins." A custodial wallet is convenient but the exchange controls the funds.

Custodial wallet: a third party (exchange or bank) holds the private keys and manages the wallet on your behalf.
Non-custodial wallet: you hold the private key/seed phrase; no third party can move the funds.

Q7Define hot wallet storage and cold wallet storage. Discuss MetaMask and its importance, benefits and drawbacks.

9 marks★ 1×Unit 4

Asked in: 2025 May Q4(b) [9]

1. Hot wallet

A hot wallet is a wallet whose private keys are stored on a device that is connected to the internet. It can sign and send transactions immediately.

  • Examples: MetaMask, Trust Wallet, exchange wallets, mobile wallets.
  • Good for: daily spending, trading, DeFi, NFTs.
  • Risk: malware, phishing, fake dApp approvals, exchange hacks.

2. Cold wallet

A cold wallet keeps the private keys offline. The key is generated and stored on a device that never touches the internet, or on paper.

  • Examples: Ledger, Trezor, air-gapped laptop, paper wallet.
  • Good for: long-term savings ("HODL"), large amounts.
  • Risk: physical loss, fire/water damage, forgotten PIN, no quick trades.

3. Hot vs cold comparison

PointHot walletCold wallet
Internet connectionAlways onlineOffline
Key storagePhone, browser, exchange serverHardware device / paper
SpeedInstantSlow (plug in, confirm)
SecurityLowerHigher
ConvenienceHighLow
Typical amountSpending moneySavings / large holdings
ExamplesMetaMask, Trust WalletLedger, Trezor, paper wallet

4. MetaMask — importance, benefits and drawbacks

MetaMask is a free, non-custodial wallet and DApp browser made by ConsenSys. It runs as a browser extension (Chrome, Firefox, Brave, Edge) and a mobile app. It stores the seed phrase locally and connects to Ethereum and EVM-compatible networks.

Benefits

  • Self-custody of keys.
  • One-click connection to DApps, DeFi and NFT marketplaces.
  • Supports ETH, ERC-20 tokens and EVM chains (Polygon, BNB, Arbitrum).
  • Built-in swap and bridge.
  • Custom gas fee and network settings.
  • Free and open source; huge user base.

Drawbacks

  • Hot wallet: exposed to phishing and malicious approvals.
  • No recovery if the seed phrase is lost.
  • Ethereum-centric; no native Bitcoin support.
  • Fake MetaMask extensions and fake airdrops are common.
  • Users must understand gas and network switching.

4.5 MetaMask in detail

Q8What is MetaMask? Illustrate in detail. Discuss its features and importance.

5 / 6 / 9 marks★★★★★ 5×Unit 4

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q3(b) [6] · 2023 Nov Q3(c) [5] · 2024 May Q3(a) [9] · 2025 May Q4(b) [9, part] · 2025 Nov Q3(a) [9]

1. Definition

MetaMask is a non-custodial cryptocurrency wallet and gateway to blockchain apps. It was launched in 2016 by ConsenSys. It is available as a browser extension and a mobile app. It generates and stores the user's private keys on the device, and it lets the user interact with Ethereum and EVM-compatible networks.

2. Why it is needed

  • A normal browser is centralised; it has no wallet and cannot sign blockchain transactions.
  • DApps need a bridge between the web page and the blockchain.
  • Users need to hold ether to pay gas.
  • Users need a safe place for ERC-20 tokens and NFTs.

3. Key features (2024 May Q3a — 9 marks)

Non-custodial key storageThe seed phrase and private keys stay on the user's device. ConsenSys cannot move funds.

4. How to set up and use MetaMask

  1. Install the extension from the official site or the mobile app from the official store.
  2. Create a new wallet; set a strong password.
  3. Write the 12-word seed phrase on paper and store it offline.
  4. Fund the account with ETH (from an exchange or another wallet).
  5. Connect to a DApp; approve the connection request.
  6. Confirm the transaction and gas fee in the MetaMask pop-up.
  7. Track the transaction on Etherscan.

5. Importance

  • It is the default entry point to Ethereum DeFi, NFTs and DAOs.
  • It makes self-custody usable for non-technical people.
  • It standardised the DApp connection flow (eth_requestAccounts).
  • It supports multiple EVM chains with one wallet.
  • It is free and has a large developer ecosystem.

4.8 Bitcoin in detail

Q11Explain in detail Bitcoin. / What is bitcoin? / Explain the concept of Bitcoin in Blockchain Technology.

4 / 5 marks★★★★ 4×Unit 4

Asked in: 2022 Nov Q4(c) [5] · 2023 Nov Q4(c) [5] · 2024 May Q1(c) [4] · 2025 Nov Q1(c) [4]

1. Definition

Bitcoin (BTC) is the first cryptocurrency and a peer-to-peer electronic cash system. It was described in a 2008 whitepaper by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto and released as open-source software in 2009. It lets two parties transfer value directly without a bank or other intermediary.

2. Key facts

3. How Bitcoin works

  1. A user creates a wallet with a public address and a private key.
  2. To pay, the user signs a transaction that spends one or more UTXOs.
  3. The transaction is broadcast to the peer-to-peer network and waits in the mempool.
  4. Miners select transactions, build a block and solve the PoW puzzle.
  5. The winning miner broadcasts the block; other nodes verify it.
  6. The block is appended to the chain; the receiver sees confirmations.
  7. After several confirmations, the payment is treated as final.

4. UTXO model (one-mark differentiator)

There are no "balances" inside Bitcoin. A wallet balance is the sum of all unspent outputs that the wallet can unlock. When you pay 0.6 BTC from a 1 BTC UTXO, the transaction creates a 0.6 BTC output to the receiver and a ~0.4 BTC change output back to you (minus fee).

5. Significance of Bitcoin (5-mark version)

  • First successful solution to the double-spending problem without a central authority.
  • Enables cheap international payments and censorship-resistant value transfer.
  • Fixed supply makes it deflationary; often called "digital gold".
  • Open-source and auditable by anyone.
  • Introduced the blockchain data structure and PoW mining.

7. Advantages and limitations

Advantages

  • Decentralised — no central bank.
  • Fixed supply, inflation-resistant.
  • Borderless and censorship-resistant.
  • Transparent, immutable ledger.
  • Divisible to 8 decimals.

Limitations

  • High price volatility.
  • Slow (~7 tx/s) and energy-heavy.
  • Irreversible transactions; lost keys are gone.
  • Pseudonymous, not fully private.
  • Regulatory uncertainty.

One-line memory hooks

  • Crypto = digital money secured by cryptography on a decentralised ledger.
  • Coin = own blockchain; token = built on another chain.
  • Stablecoin = pegged to fiat (USDT). Privacy coin = hides details (XMR, ZEC).
  • Custodial = exchange holds keys. Non-custodial = you hold keys.
  • Hot = online, convenient. Cold = offline, safe.
  • MetaMask = non-custodial Ethereum wallet + DApp gateway.
  • Coinbase = regulated, beginner-friendly. Binance = low fees, many coins.
  • Zerocoin hides sender. Zerocash hides sender + receiver + amount.
  • Not your keys, not your coins.
